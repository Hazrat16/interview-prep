## 3. Teamwork & communication

### Tell me about a conflict / disagreement with a teammate

> At SmartCrowd, a teammate and I disagreed about how to build a shared UI component. They wanted a quick page-specific version, and I wanted a reusable one because similar UI was duplicated in several places. Instead of arguing, I listed the trade-offs, the time needed now versus the duplication later, and built a small proof of concept. We agreed to build it reusable, but with a smaller scope first so we didn't delay the release. That approach became part of our 20+ reusable components. What I learned is to bring evidence and agree on the goal first, not opinions.

### Disagreement with your manager or lead

> Once my lead wanted to ship a feature with a known edge case to meet a date. I explained the risk with a concrete example of how a user could hit it. We agreed to release on time but add a guard and a follow-up ticket for the full fix. I respect the final decision. My job is to make sure the risk is visible.

### How do you work with designers and product managers?

> I involve them early. During the Share Transfer Facility, I reviewed designs before implementation and raised edge cases such as partial transfers, failed payments and empty states, so they were decided before we coded. During development I shared progress on staging so feedback came early, not at the end.

### How do you handle code review feedback?

> I see it as free learning. If I agree, I fix it. If I don't, I explain my reasoning and we discuss it. Early in my career, a reviewer pointed out that I was putting business logic inside components, and that changed how I structure code. I now keep logic in hooks and services.

### How do you give code review feedback?

> I'm specific and explain the why, not only the what. I separate blocking issues, like bugs or security problems, from small suggestions, and I mention good things too. With junior engineers I sometimes pair on the fix instead of leaving ten comments.

### Tell me about mentoring someone

> At SmartCrowd I helped junior engineers through code reviews and pairing. One junior was struggling with state management and repeated API calls. I walked them through our patterns, shared examples and reviewed their PRs closely for a few weeks. After that their PRs needed far fewer changes, and they started building features independently.

### How do you communicate in a remote team?

> I've worked remotely with a Dubai-based team for over three years. I write clear updates, raise blockers early instead of waiting for stand-up, document decisions in tickets or PRs, and record a short video or screenshots when something is easier to show than explain.

### Tell me about working with a difficult person

> I try to understand why they behave that way first, usually pressure or unclear expectations. With one stakeholder who kept changing requirements, I started confirming decisions in writing after each discussion and sharing small demos often. The changes reduced because they could see progress early.

## 4. Ownership, pressure & mistakes

### What is the most challenging project you've worked on?

Use the **registration flow redesign** (full version in [star-stories.md](star-stories.md)):

> After regulators DFSA and DIFC reviewed our investor onboarding, they asked for several compliance changes before approval, with a tight deadline. I owned the frontend: I split a crowded form into multiple steps, added the new fields and validation, built document upload and request flows, and kept it working for existing users registered under the old flow. We delivered on time and the flow was approved.

### Project you're most proud of

> Professionally, the Share Transfer Facility at SmartCrowd. It let investors sell their shares to other investors, which was the platform's main liquidity feature. I led its delivery with the backend and design teams, and it launched with no major production issues. From my own projects, SmartJobHub, because I built everything from the app to the AWS infrastructure.

### Tell me about a complex bug you fixed / something critical you built

See [complex-bug-and-critical-work.md](complex-bug-and-critical-work.md).

### Tight deadline / missed deadline

> For the registration redesign we had very little time because regulatory approval depended on it. I broke the work into small parts, did the compliance-critical changes first, and told my lead early which nice-to-haves would move to the next release. We shipped the required changes on time. I learned that early communication about scope is better than late surprises.

### Tell me about a mistake you made

> Early at SmartCrowd, I shipped a change that worked on my screen but broke the layout in Arabic RTL mode, because I only tested in English. It was caught quickly and I fixed it the same day. After that I added RTL checks to my testing routine, and later we centralized RTL handling so individual pages didn't need special fixes. Now I test edge cases like language, roles and screen sizes before opening a PR.

(Replace this with your own real mistake if you have a better one. Keep it small, owned and fixed.)

### How do you handle changing requirements?

> I expect them, especially in fintech, where regulators can change things. I ask why the requirement changed so I understand the goal, estimate the impact, and agree with the PM on what moves. In the registration redesign, requirements changed after the work was done, and I restructured the flow while keeping the old path working for existing users.

### How do you prioritize multiple tasks?

> By impact and urgency. Production bugs affecting users come first, then work that blocks others, then features in sprint order. If two things are both urgent, I ask my lead or PM rather than guessing, and I communicate what will be delayed.

### Time you went beyond your role

> At SmartCrowd, the app's initial load was slow and nobody owned that problem. I analysed the bundle, introduced route-based code splitting and lazy loading, and reworked heavy legacy components. Initial load time dropped by 30 to 45 percent. This was one of the reasons for my promotion.

### How do you learn a new technology quickly?

> Official docs first, then a small project, then use it in something real. For example, I learned Terraform by building a VPC and EC2 module, then used it to deploy SmartJobHub on ECS with staging and production. At Firmwide I learned Microsoft Graph and MSAL the same way and shipped SSO.

### Tell me about a time you failed

> In one of my early side projects I tried to build too many features at once and never finished deploying it. I learned to ship a small working version first and then improve it. Since then my projects, like SmartJobHub, go live early with CI/CD and grow step by step.

### How do you handle stress / pressure?

> I break the problem into small steps and focus on the next one. I also communicate early: if something is at risk, I tell people while there is still time to adjust. During production issues I fix or roll back first, then investigate calmly.
